The winter came early that year, and with it the silence. Mara stood at the window watching the road for a rider who would not come before the first snow.

The house had been her mother's, and her mother's mother's before that, and every board of it creaked with an opinion of its own.

2

Spring was only a rumor by then. The petitions had gone unanswered for three long seasons, and the magistrate's clerk arrived on a Tuesday with nothing in his hands.

3

By summer the road was busy again, though not with the traffic anyone had hoped for, and Mara had learned to read a stranger's boots before she read his face.
